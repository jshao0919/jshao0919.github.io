Tires and wheels: inspect without creating a hazard

Recognize common tire and wheel observations, use the proper inspection method, and leave specialized work to qualified personnel.

Learning objectives

  • Describe tire damage and wheel concerns precisely.
  • Explain why pressure and torque values must come from the applicable procedure.
  • Recognize wheel-assembly stored-energy hazards.

Start with position and visible condition

Identify the exact wheel position using the vehicle’s terminology. Inspect the visible tread, sidewall, valve area, and surrounding components as the TM directs. Look for the kinds of damage and interference the procedure identifies. A tire that appears inflated can still be outside its specified condition or pressure. Do not reach into a tight or unsafe space to see the hidden side of a wheel.

  1. Secure the vehicle and choose a safe viewing position.
  2. Identify the wheel position unambiguously.
  3. Inspect the accessible surfaces using the required method.
  4. Note the location and appearance of damage or contact.
  5. Use the TM’s criteria to determine the required reporting or maintenance action.

Pressure is a measurement, not an estimate

The required tire pressure and measurement conditions depend on the tire, vehicle, configuration, and procedure. Use the specified gauge and method. A generic training value is not an acceptable substitute. A central tire inflation indication, if fitted, must be interpreted under that system’s instructions. Inflation or deflation is a separate action that requires authorization and the correct safe procedure.

  1. Find the correct pressure instruction for the installed assembly and use conditions.
  2. Confirm the required measurement state and gauge suitability.
  3. Measure only as the TM and your training permit.
  4. Record the actual value and unit when required.
  5. Report a discrepancy and use the authorized adjustment or repair process.

Wheel hardware and witness marks

Inspect accessible wheel hardware and related indicators as the TM specifies. Missing hardware, visible movement, unusual staining, damaged components, or a changed witness mark can require investigation. A witness mark is only evidence of relative movement; it does not prove that the assembly has the correct torque. Do not use an improvised tightening sequence or a remembered torque value.

  1. Observe the hardware without disturbing it during a visual check.
  2. Compare the condition with the applicable inspection criteria.
  3. Record which position and component is affected.
  4. Refer required torque verification or repair to the authorized procedure and personnel.
  5. Ensure corrective work is documented and checked as required.

Treat wheel assemblies as stored-energy systems

Tire and wheel assemblies can release energy violently. Damaged wheels, multi-piece assemblies, inflation work, and disassembly require specific training and equipment. Do not loosen assembly hardware, apply heat, or attempt an improvised repair. The safe response to a suspected dangerous assembly is to keep people clear and use the established maintenance process.

  1. Recognize that inspection does not authorize disassembly.
  2. Do not approach or handle an assembly showing a condition identified as hazardous by the procedure.
  3. Keep others away from the hazard as required.
  4. Notify qualified personnel and explain the observation.
  5. Follow the approved isolation, handling, and repair instructions.

Write a tire or wheel fault that can be found

A useful entry identifies the vehicle location, component, observed condition, and applicable inspection reference. Avoid “bad tire” or “wheel loose” unless looseness was actually established by an authorized check. A drawing or photograph may supplement the record if unit rules allow it, but it does not replace the required written entry.

  1. Name the exact wheel position.
  2. State what you saw or measured.
  3. Include the required PMCS item or technical reference.
  4. Record any relevant measurement with its unit and method context.
  5. Report the condition for status determination under the TM.
Worked example

Training wording: “Left front tire sidewall has visible damage near the outer shoulder; condition referred for evaluation against the applicable TM criteria.” Add actual measurements only if the procedure required and you performed them.

Keep these habits

  • Vehicle-specific pressure and torque values come from the correct technical data.
  • Witness marks do not verify torque.
  • Do not turn a visual inspection into unauthorized tire or wheel work.

Read alongside: The technical manual for the exact vehicle and configuration; DA Pam 750–1 — maintenance management guidance; DA Pam 750–8 — The Army Maintenance Management System (TAMMS) Users Manual.
