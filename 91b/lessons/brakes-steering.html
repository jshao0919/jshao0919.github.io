Brakes, steering, and suspension observations

Recognize what needs inspection and reporting without inventing universal adjustment or rejection limits.

Learning objectives

  • Follow the prescribed method for safety-critical checks.
  • Distinguish visual observations from authorized functional tests.
  • Report abnormal braking or steering behavior without unsafe experiments.

Begin with the applicable system

Vehicles use different brake, steering, and suspension arrangements. First identify the system and read its inspection instructions. Do not transfer an adjustment method, pressure value, travel limit, or test sequence from another vehicle. A familiar control layout does not establish that the underlying system is the same.

  1. Confirm vehicle configuration and the relevant PMCS items.
  2. Read the system’s warnings and setup requirements.
  3. Identify which checks are assigned to your role.
  4. Prepare the required equipment and safe work area.
  5. Refer unclear instructions or conflicting symptoms to a qualified maintainer.

Inspect accessible components safely

Visual checks may address accessible lines, hoses, connections, mountings, and other components identified in the TM. Look for the specified kinds of damage, leakage, interference, or missing hardware. Do not place yourself under an unsupported vehicle or put fingers between components that can move. A visual check cannot establish an internal condition that requires a specified test.

  1. Establish the required safe vehicle state.
  2. Use a safe viewing position for each specified component.
  3. Observe condition without disturbing an adjustment or opening a line.
  4. Record the exact location and visible condition.
  5. Refer required measurements or tests to the applicable procedure.

Conduct only the prescribed functional check

A functional check asks whether a system responds as specified under controlled conditions. It may require an operator, an observer, a prepared area, or particular initial conditions. Do not improvise a road test, defeat a warning, or adjust a component until a symptom disappears. A successful response during one check does not cancel an unresolved fault from another check.

  1. Read the complete check, including its stop criteria, before starting.
  2. Confirm the required personnel, area, and initial conditions.
  3. Perform the specified actions in order.
  4. Compare the observed response with the stated criteria.
  5. Stop and report any unexpected or unacceptable response.

Describe the symptom without prescribing a repair

Examples of useful observations include a specific warning indication, visible leakage at a stated location, unusual steering effort, or a reported braking change. “Replace the pump” is a repair recommendation, not a description of what the operator observed. Diagnosis requires the authorized troubleshooting process and may reveal a different cause.

  1. Identify the affected function and the observed symptom.
  2. Include when the symptom occurred and what was happening.
  3. Record actual readings only when obtained by the required method.
  4. Avoid naming a failed part unless it has been confirmed.
  5. Report the condition for maintenance evaluation and status determination.
Worked example

Training wording: “Operator reports steering effort increased during authorized operation; cause not determined. Vehicle referred for maintenance evaluation.”

Do not use a defect symbol as permission

Recording a status symbol communicates a condition through the maintenance system; it does not independently authorize movement. Use the applicable TM criteria, current forms instructions, and designated review process. Do not reduce the severity of an entry because a short movement seems convenient or because the vehicle completed its last trip.

  1. Document the observed condition accurately.
  2. Apply the required criteria and obtain review when uncertain.
  3. Keep operation within the current authorized status and restrictions.
  4. Complete authorized repair and required verification.
  5. Have the designated personnel complete any status or release action.

Keep these habits

  • Safety-critical systems require their own procedures and limits.
  • Functional checks need defined conditions and stop criteria.
  • A symptom is evidence for troubleshooting, not proof of a failed part.

Read alongside: The technical manual for the exact vehicle and configuration; DA Pam 750–8 — The Army Maintenance Management System (TAMMS) Users Manual; AR 750–1 — Army Materiel Maintenance Policy.
