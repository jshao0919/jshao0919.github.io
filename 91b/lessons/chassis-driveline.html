Chassis, driveline, body, and mounted equipment

Inspect accessible structural and driveline items, verify secure stowage, and understand the limits of a visual check.

Learning objectives

  • Recognize accessible conditions that need documentation.
  • Use safe access for underbody and raised-component checks.
  • Distinguish lubrication instructions from a general request to add grease.

Inspect structure and mountings systematically

The applicable PMCS may include frame, brackets, mounts, body panels, access doors, and other structural items. Observe the condition and security specified by the TM. A crack-like mark, displaced part, missing fastener, or unusual contact requires accurate documentation and evaluation; do not assume a mark is harmless or diagnose a structural failure from appearance alone.

  1. Identify each required item and a safe viewing position.
  2. Observe the specified surfaces, attachments, and clearances.
  3. Record the location and appearance of an abnormal condition.
  4. Avoid removing hardware or disturbing an assembly during a visual check.
  5. Refer required measurements or repair to the correct technical procedure.

Underbody access and rotating driveline components

Driveshafts and other rotating components create entanglement hazards. Inspect only under the vehicle state specified by the procedure, with required movement and energy controls. Never reach near a rotating shaft or place yourself beneath an unsupported vehicle. A visual inspection may identify a visible leak, damaged guard, or missing hardware; it does not establish internal wear or adjustment.

  1. Confirm safe access and the required vehicle state.
  2. Inspect only the accessible items assigned by the TM.
  3. Stay clear of pinch points, hot components, and stored energy.
  4. Describe visible conditions without guessing internal damage.
  5. Use authorized troubleshooting for noise, vibration, or other symptoms.

Lubrication is a specified service

The correct lubrication instruction identifies the product, point, interval, preparation, and method. Applying more lubricant is not automatically better, and the wrong product can damage equipment. Some points have special access or operating-state requirements. Use the lubrication order or applicable technical instruction; do not create a universal grease schedule from a generic vehicle picture.

  1. Identify the correct lubrication publication and applicable configuration.
  2. Verify the point, product, interval, and task assignment.
  3. Prepare the area and dispensing equipment as directed.
  4. Perform the specified service without overapplication or contamination.
  5. Record service completion and any inaccessible or damaged points as required.

Body, stowage, and required equipment

Loose tools and unsecured equipment can injure occupants or interfere with controls. Inspect stowage, retaining devices, access closures, and required on-vehicle equipment according to the applicable list and PMCS. Verify presence and condition rather than assuming a closed compartment is complete. Do not add improvised restraints or modify a mounting without authorization.

  1. Use the applicable inventory and inspection instructions.
  2. Check required items for presence and visible serviceability.
  3. Verify that stowage and closures are secured as directed.
  4. Record missing, damaged, or insecure items accurately.
  5. Route shortages and defects through the required supply and maintenance process.

Towing and recovery attachments are inspected, not improvised

A PMCS may include accessible towing or recovery attachments and related hardware. Inspect only as the TM assigns and document damage or missing parts. Recognizing an attachment does not establish its capacity or authorize a recovery operation. Actual towing, lifting, and recovery require their own procedures, ratings, equipment, and trained personnel.

  1. Identify the attachment and its assigned inspection task.
  2. Observe the specified mounting, hardware, and visible condition.
  3. Record any abnormal condition by exact location.
  4. Prevent use of questionable equipment through the required reporting process.
  5. Refer operational use to the applicable authorized procedure.

Keep these habits

  • Visual checks reveal evidence; they do not prove internal condition.
  • Lubrication requires the specified product, point, method, and interval.
  • Secure stowage and complete equipment checks are part of readiness.
