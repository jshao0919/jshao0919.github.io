During and after operation: notice, stop, and follow through

Observe vehicle behavior safely, record useful context, and complete the required checks after use.

Learning objectives

  • Monitor required indications without distracting the operator.
  • Describe an abnormal event with useful operating context.
  • Complete after-operation records and handoff.

During-operation PMCS is continuous attention

During-operation checks focus on required indications and vehicle behavior while the vehicle is being used. Examples include instrument indications, unusual noise, vibration, steering response, and braking behavior when the applicable procedure calls for those observations. They do not justify unsafe experiments. The operator must maintain safe control and attention; record detailed notes only when it is safe to do so.

  1. Know the required indications and operating precautions before moving.
  2. Observe the items specified by the TM during normal authorized use.
  3. Recognize a change from the expected condition.
  4. Follow the TM and operating procedure if an abnormal indication appears.
  5. Record the event once the vehicle and people are safe.

Respond to an abnormal indication

An unexpected warning, smoke, smell, loss of response, or unusual vibration requires a deliberate response. Do not continue simply to see whether it becomes worse. Use the vehicle’s published response procedure and the local emergency or recovery process as applicable. This course does not prescribe a universal shutdown sequence because the safe action depends on the system and situation.

  1. Maintain control and use the applicable immediate-action procedure.
  2. Bring the vehicle and work area to the required safe condition.
  3. Notify the responsible person and identify the vehicle.
  4. Describe the indication and circumstances without guessing a failed component.
  5. Do not resume operation until the required evaluation and authorization are complete.

Capture the context a mechanic needs

A fault that occurs only under certain conditions can be difficult to reproduce. Useful context includes when it began, what the vehicle was doing, which indication changed, and whether the condition continued after stopping. Record available readings accurately with units. Do not invent a number from memory, deliberately recreate a hazardous event, or state that a component failed when you only observed a symptom.

  1. Record the date and the relevant meter reading through the required process.
  2. Name the location, indicator, or function involved.
  3. State the operating condition in ordinary language.
  4. Record the observed effect and any action taken.
  5. Tell the maintainer what is uncertain or was not observed.
Worked example

Useful: “During normal operation, an intermittent squeal was heard near the engine compartment; source not determined. Reported to maintenance.” This is more informative than “engine bad,” without claiming an unverified cause.

After-operation PMCS has its own instructions

After use, some conditions are easier to notice, and the vehicle needs to be prepared for its next use. Follow the after-operation table rather than repeating only the checks you remember from the morning. Some observations require a particular temperature, waiting period, or vehicle state; those conditions come from the TM. Do not open hot or pressurized components simply because the vehicle has been parked.

  1. Secure the vehicle and establish the state required by the procedure.
  2. Perform the after-operation PMCS items in their specified order.
  3. Inspect for new damage, leaks, or loose items as directed.
  4. Perform only authorized replenishment, cleaning, or other services.
  5. Complete the record and report unresolved or new faults.

Make the handoff understandable

A useful handoff connects the observed condition, the record, and the next responsible person. Explain what happened, what was checked, what remains uncertain, and whether the vehicle has been referred for review. Do not rely on a verbal comment that never reaches the worksheet or maintenance process. The next operator needs the current record, not an informal promise that a problem will be fixed later.

  1. Confirm that all required observations and meter information were entered.
  2. Point out new or changed faults to the responsible person.
  3. Identify any checks or corrective actions still incomplete.
  4. Follow the unit’s record-routing and vehicle-securing process.
  5. Verify that the handoff was received.

Keep these habits

  • Monitor required indications without compromising safe operation.
  • Context helps troubleshooting; guesses make it harder.
  • After-operation checks and a documented handoff prepare the next user.

Read alongside: The technical manual for the exact vehicle and configuration; DA Pam 750–8 — The Army Maintenance Management System (TAMMS) Users Manual; Dispatch records — DA Form 5987-E / DD Form 1970, as applicable.
