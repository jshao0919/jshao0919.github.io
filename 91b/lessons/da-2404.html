DA Form 2404: a guided paper worksheet

Practice a logical sequence for an Equipment Inspection and Maintenance Worksheet using fictional training data.

Learning objectives

  • Populate identification and inspection information from valid sources.
  • Place faults and corrective actions in their proper parts of the worksheet.
  • Respect signature, review, and correction responsibilities.

Know which worksheet you are using

DA Form 2404 is the Equipment Inspection and Maintenance Worksheet. Use the current authorized form and instructions when your unit’s process calls for it. A unit using automated maintenance records may direct a different worksheet or workflow. The course demo is a simplified learning aid with fictional information; it is not an official blank form, a dispatch authorization, or a completed maintenance record.

  1. Confirm that DA Form 2404 is appropriate for the task under the unit process.
  2. Obtain the current authorized form and completion instructions.
  3. Identify which entries belong to the inspector, maintainer, and reviewer.
  4. Gather the equipment record, current TM, and actual observations.
  5. Use only training identities and values in this public practice tool.

Step 1: identify the equipment and inspection

Complete the organization and equipment-identification information required by the form. The nomenclature describes what the item is; the model identifies its configuration; the required registration, serial, or other identifier connects the worksheet to the individual item. Enter the inspection date and type according to current instructions. Do not substitute a bumper marking or familiar nickname where another identifier is required.

  1. Read the organization and equipment information from the authorized source.
  2. Enter the required nomenclature, model, and identifying number accurately.
  3. Enter the actual inspection date in the required format.
  4. Identify the inspection type or interval correctly.
  5. Recheck the header against the vehicle and source record before writing faults.

Step 2: record readings and technical references

Enter only meter or usage readings that apply to the equipment and form instructions. Record the actual reading in the proper field with the expected units; do not manufacture a value for an inapplicable field. Identify the applicable technical publication and required date or change information exactly as instructed. This course does not assign a fictional vehicle to a real TM number.

  1. Determine which usage fields apply to the equipment.
  2. Read the applicable meter through the authorized method.
  3. Enter the reading without silently changing units or rounding beyond the instruction.
  4. Use the prescribed treatment for inapplicable fields.
  5. Enter the correct technical-publication information from the publication itself.

Step 3: write one understandable fault entry

Use the inspection-item reference, status, and deficiency or shortcoming fields according to current completion instructions. Keep each entry tied to a distinct finding so corrective work can be traced. Do not invent an item number because the simulator asks for a reference: in real work, copy the reference from the applicable PMCS table or use the authorized treatment for the type of fault.

  1. Identify the applicable PMCS item or required reference.
  2. Apply the correct status process using the TM criteria and current instructions.
  3. Write the location and observed condition clearly.
  4. Add relevant conditions or actual measurements when needed.
  5. Review the entry with the responsible person if the reference or status is uncertain.
Worked example

Training entry: “Right rear turn indicator did not illuminate during the prescribed lighting check.” The demo supplies a fictional training reference; a real worksheet requires the actual applicable reference.

Step 4: document corrective work and required certification

The corrective-action area records work actually completed against the fault. Include the required verification and identifying information through the prescribed process. The inspector, maintainer, and maintenance supervisor have distinct responsibilities; do not sign or initial for another person or complete a certification before the action occurs. Exact signature and time entries must follow the current form instructions.

  1. Keep the fault entry readable while corrective work is performed.
  2. Enter the actual authorized corrective action in the proper area.
  3. Record the required verification result.
  4. Have each responsible person complete their own required entry.
  5. Obtain required review before the record is closed or routed.

Step 5: review and route the worksheet

Review the worksheet from top to bottom. Confirm identity, date, inspection type, usage readings, publication information, item references, status, fault descriptions, and any completed corrective actions. Follow the authorized correction method and record-routing process. A neatly completed form can still be wrong if it belongs to the wrong vehicle or reports work that never happened.

  1. Compare header information with the source record.
  2. Match every fault to its reference and any corrective action.
  3. Check that required entries are complete and readable.
  4. Resolve errors through the approved correction process.
  5. Submit and retain the record through the unit’s maintenance system.

Keep these habits

  • Confirm the authorized form and instructions before entering real data.
  • Header accuracy connects the inspection to the correct equipment.
  • Faults, corrective actions, and certifications have different purposes and owners.

Read alongside: DA Form 2404 — Equipment Inspection and Maintenance Worksheet; DA Pam 750–8 — The Army Maintenance Management System (TAMMS) Users Manual; The technical manual for the exact vehicle and configuration.
