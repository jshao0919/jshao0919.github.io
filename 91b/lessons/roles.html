Start with the vehicle and its manual

Understand what a 91B does, where PMCS fits, and how to find the instruction that applies to the vehicle in front of you.

Learning objectives

  • Separate operator checks from maintainer work and release authority.
  • Match equipment identity to the correct technical publication.
  • Read a PMCS table without confusing a check with a rejection criterion.

What PMCS accomplishes

Preventive maintenance checks and services (PMCS) are organized inspections and services used to find problems early, preserve equipment, and document its condition. A check asks you to look, listen, measure, or verify something. A service directs an action such as lubrication or replacement when the publication authorizes it. A quick walk around is useful, but it is not automatically a complete PMCS. The applicable technical manual (TM) tells you what work is required and when.

  1. Identify the vehicle and the inspection interval you are performing.
  2. Use the applicable PMCS table in its stated order.
  3. Perform each authorized check using its instructions and precautions.
  4. Record findings that require documentation and report conditions that prevent safe completion.
Worked example

Seeing that a tire is present does not complete a tire check. The TM may require condition, inflation, wheel hardware, and other observations using specified methods.

Operator, maintainer, and supervisor roles

An operator conducts the checks and services assigned to the operator and reports faults. A 91B wheeled vehicle mechanic inspects, troubleshoots, repairs, and services equipment within training, authorization, and the maintenance allocation. A supervisor coordinates work, reviews documentation, and applies the relevant maintenance controls. A dispatch or release decision belongs to the designated authority. Finding a problem does not automatically authorize a repair, a status change, or operation of the vehicle.

  1. Read who the procedure is written for and what maintenance level it assigns.
  2. Check that required training, tools, and authorization are available.
  3. Refer work outside your assignment to the appropriate maintainer or supervisor.
  4. Treat completion of a repair and authorization to operate as separate decisions.

Identify the equipment before opening a procedure

A family name or familiar appearance may not identify the exact configuration. Model, serial or registration identifiers, installed equipment, and applicable modifications can change the instruction. Use equipment records and identification plates according to local procedure. Do not infer an exact model from a generic drawing in this course. The truck in the demonstrations is an illustration, not a vehicle-specific maintenance reference.

  1. Read the equipment identification required by your work package or record.
  2. Match the model and configuration to the publication applicability statement.
  3. Check publication date, changes, warnings, and relevant supplemental instructions.
  4. If identity or applicability is uncertain, resolve that uncertainty before performing the procedure.

Read the whole PMCS entry

PMCS tables commonly separate an item number, interval, location or item to check, procedure, and a column describing conditions under which equipment is not ready or available. Titles vary by manual. Read across the entire row, including notes and references. An item number is a navigation aid: it is not a vehicle fault code or a universal reference shared by every TM.

  1. Locate the interval and the item in the correct table.
  2. Read warnings, cautions, and notes before starting.
  3. Follow the procedure and any referenced work package.
  4. Compare the actual observation with the applicable criteria.
  5. Record the correct reference and observation; escalate uncertainty instead of inventing a limit.
Worked example

“Inspect for leaks” tells you what to observe. The associated criteria and fluid-system instructions determine what the finding means for that vehicle.

Prepare a reference trail

A useful record lets another person find the instruction you used. Keep the publication identifier, applicable change information, and relevant table, work package, or item available. Use the exact reference format required by the form and local process. This course supplies general examples and links to publication discovery pages; it does not verify the currently effective edition of every Army publication.

  1. Obtain the authorized current publication through your unit’s publication process.
  2. Keep the relevant instruction available at the job, rather than relying on memory.
  3. Record the required publication identification accurately.
  4. When the form and the TM appear inconsistent, ask the maintenance supervisor to resolve the conflict.

Keep these habits

  • The applicable vehicle TM supplies the actual procedure and limits.
  • A mechanic’s skill does not replace authorization or maintenance allocation.
  • Read every PMCS row as a complete instruction, including its criteria.

Read alongside: The technical manual for the exact vehicle and configuration; AR 750–1 — Army Materiel Maintenance Policy; DA Pam 750–1 — maintenance management guidance.
