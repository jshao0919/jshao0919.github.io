Scheduled services and the maintenance workflow

Connect inspection, troubleshooting, parts, repair, verification, and records into one traceable job.

Learning objectives

  • Distinguish PMCS intervals from scheduled-service requirements.
  • Track a fault through an authorized maintenance process.
  • Explain why repair completion requires verification and record updates.

Intervals are instructions, not suggestions

Before, during, after, weekly, monthly, and service-based intervals appear only where the applicable publication requires them. Scheduled work may depend on time, usage, condition, or other specified criteria. Do not assume that every vehicle has the same schedule or that a low-mileage vehicle has no service due. Use the maintenance system and technical data together to identify what is required.

  1. Identify the applicable inspection and service schedules.
  2. Verify dates, meter readings, and other scheduling information.
  3. Review upcoming or overdue work with the responsible person.
  4. Plan the required personnel, tools, materials, and work area.
  5. Record completion or an unresolved scheduling discrepancy through the authorized process.

From observation to work assignment

A fault begins as evidence: a visible condition, a measured result, or an operating symptom. The record then supports review, prioritization, and assignment. The exact digital and paper workflow varies by unit and system configuration. Keep the original observation understandable so the assigned maintainer does not have to reconstruct what the operator meant.

  1. Document the finding and required reference.
  2. Submit it through the unit’s maintenance reporting process.
  3. Have the designated personnel review status and work requirements.
  4. Assign work to qualified personnel using the required work documentation.
  5. Preserve the connection between the original fault and later corrective actions.

Troubleshoot before replacing parts

A troubleshooting procedure narrows possible causes through specified observations and tests. Follow its sequence and safety instructions. Replacing the most familiar part may waste time and leave the fault unresolved. Record test results accurately, including required units and conditions, so the next maintainer can understand what has been ruled in or out.

  1. Confirm the reported symptom using an authorized method when safe.
  2. Find the applicable troubleshooting procedure.
  3. Complete each required test in order with suitable equipment.
  4. Use the stated result to choose the next authorized step.
  5. Document the confirmed cause or unresolved condition without inventing a result.

Parts and deferred work need traceability

A requested part is not an installed part, and an installed part is not proof that the fault is corrected. Preserve the link between the fault, work requirement, and parts or support action. When work cannot be completed, use the current authorized process for open or deferred faults. Do not delete or close a fault simply because a part is on order.

  1. Identify the required part or support through authorized technical and supply data.
  2. Record the request or work status through the designated system.
  3. Keep the fault visible until the required corrective and verification actions are complete.
  4. Use authorized deferred-maintenance records only when applicable.
  5. Communicate what remains pending during each handoff.

Repair, verify, and close the record

Corrective action should state what was actually done. Verification should state the check performed and its result, as required by the task and records process. “Fixed” alone gives the next person little information. Required inspections, supervisor reviews, and status changes must be completed by the designated personnel. Never pre-sign a job or enter completion before the work occurs.

  1. Perform the authorized repair or service using the applicable instruction.
  2. Conduct the specified operational, quality, or other verification check.
  3. Record the actual corrective action and verification result.
  4. Obtain required reviews, signatures, or system actions from the responsible people.
  5. Update equipment status and records through the authorized workflow.

Clean closeout supports the next job

Account for tools, restore guards and access covers as required, remove test equipment, and address waste or spills through approved procedures. Verify that the work area and vehicle are left in the specified condition. A complete record and clear handoff help prevent repeat work, missed faults, and assumptions about what was completed.

  1. Account for tools and removed or replaced items.
  2. Restore the configuration specified by the procedure.
  3. Handle waste, used fluids, and contaminated materials correctly.
  4. Review the record for incomplete entries and unresolved faults.
  5. Brief the next responsible person on the final condition and remaining work.

Keep these habits

  • Use the actual schedule and meter data for the vehicle.
  • A parts request does not close a fault.
  • Repair, verification, record completion, and release are distinct steps.
