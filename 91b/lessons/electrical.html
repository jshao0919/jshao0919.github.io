Batteries, wiring, lights, and electrical symptoms

Inspect accessible electrical items, recognize battery hazards, and document electrical faults without unsafe trial-and-error work.

Learning objectives

  • Identify common battery and electrical inspection hazards.
  • Separate visible evidence from an electrical diagnosis.
  • Use prescribed test equipment and connection procedures.

Electrical power can remain present

Turning off a control does not necessarily isolate every circuit. Batteries, stored energy, and alternate power paths may still present hazards. Jewelry and conductive tools can create a short circuit. Follow the task’s power-control instructions and use appropriate protective equipment. Do not disconnect a cable or remove a cover merely because the engine is stopped.

  1. Read the electrical warnings and initial setup for the task.
  2. Identify the required power state and energy controls.
  3. Remove or control conductive items as the procedure requires.
  4. Use the specified tools and safe access method.
  5. Verify the required state before hands-on work.

Battery inspection begins with condition and restraint

Battery tasks can involve corrosive material, gas, high current, and heavy components. Inspect the items assigned by the TM, such as accessible case condition, restraint, connections, and protective covers. Avoid sparks and ignition sources. A swollen, cracked, leaking, hot, or otherwise abnormal battery requires the procedure’s response and qualified review; do not experiment with charging or connection changes.

  1. Prepare the work area and protective equipment specified for the task.
  2. Observe accessible battery and mounting conditions safely.
  3. Record damage, leakage, corrosion, or insecure components as required.
  4. Use the approved response for an abnormal battery condition.
  5. Perform cleaning, servicing, removal, or charging only under the applicable procedure and authorization.

Wiring and connections: describe what is visible

Accessible wiring checks can reveal abrasion, damaged insulation, insecure routing, missing protection, or a visibly disconnected connector. Do not pull on a harness to “test” it, pierce insulation without an authorized procedure, or reconnect an unknown connector by guesswork. Electrical faults may be intermittent, and damage location matters to troubleshooting.

  1. Identify the harness or component location using the TM terminology.
  2. Observe routing, protection, and visible condition as directed.
  3. Keep clear of energized or moving components.
  4. Record the specific visible defect and location.
  5. Refer continuity, voltage, or other testing to the authorized procedure.

Lights and indicators need a defined check

A lighting check is more useful when it states which lamp, selected function, and observed response were involved. Confirm the required switch position and test conditions using the TM. A lamp that does not illuminate may have several possible causes. Do not immediately replace parts or bypass a protective device without troubleshooting and authorization.

  1. Prepare the vehicle and observer position for the specified check.
  2. Select the required function according to the procedure.
  3. Observe each required lamp or indication.
  4. Record the exact function and location that did not respond correctly.
  5. Follow the approved troubleshooting and repair process.
Worked example

Training wording: “Right rear turn indicator did not illuminate during the prescribed lighting check; left rear indicator illuminated.” This is more useful than “electrical system broken.”

Meters, protective devices, and starting support

Use test equipment with the correct rating, condition, range, and connection method specified for the task. Incorrect connections can damage equipment or injure personnel. Never bridge a fuse or circuit-protection device to make a system operate. External starting support, battery connection order, and charging procedures are vehicle- and equipment-specific tasks; use the exact authorized instructions and trained personnel.

  1. Confirm that testing or starting support is part of the authorized task.
  2. Inspect the required equipment and verify its suitability.
  3. Read the complete connection and disconnection sequence.
  4. Perform the procedure within its stated limits and precautions.
  5. Record the result and remove test equipment through the prescribed sequence.

Keep these habits

  • Engine off does not mean every electrical circuit is safe to touch.
  • Report the exact lamp, harness location, or indication involved.
  • Do not bypass protection or improvise battery and test-equipment connections.

Read alongside: The technical manual for the exact vehicle and configuration; DA Pam 750–1 — maintenance management guidance; DA Pam 750–8 — The Army Maintenance Management System (TAMMS) Users Manual.
