Capstone: inspect, document, and hand off

Bring the course together in a fictional vehicle inspection with clear observations, correct record choices, and an honest maintenance handoff.

Learning objectives

  • Complete a structured inspection without skipping safety or record review.
  • Produce a clear training worksheet from observed facts.
  • Explain what must happen before a fault is closed or operation is authorized.

Scenario: a fictional training vehicle

You are assigned to help inspect a fictional wheeled training vehicle. The worksheet contains one open fault, and the inspection reveals several observations. The simulator supplies its own training references and criteria so you can practice the reasoning process. Those references are invented for the exercise and must never be copied onto an actual vehicle record. The drawing has no real model-specific component locations.

  1. Read the training vehicle identity and inspection objective.
  2. Review the supplied existing fault and the exercise instructions.
  3. Identify your role and which decisions require review.
  4. Open the fictional training reference for each demonstrated item.
  5. Prepare to record evidence without adding facts not supplied by the scenario.

Phase 1: prepare and inspect

Before selecting an inspection station, explain the safe work state and what you are trying to observe. Work through the inspection route deliberately. For each observation, decide whether you have enough information to describe it, whether the check is complete, and whether it requires a stop or referral under the supplied exercise criteria. Do not reward yourself for guessing a component failure.

  1. Complete the safe-setup review.
  2. Inspect each assigned training item in order.
  3. Read the observation and relevant exercise criteria.
  4. Distinguish a completed check from one that requires additional authorized work.
  5. Record new findings and report any condition requiring immediate review.

Phase 2: write the record

Choose the worksheet format the exercise assigns. Enter the fictional identification and inspection information, then write each fault using a precise location and observed condition. Use only the supplied training references and stated readings. If a scenario does not establish a diagnosis, your entry should not claim one. The form tutor gives feedback on completeness and meaning; it does not validate a real maintenance record.

  1. Match the worksheet to the fictional vehicle.
  2. Enter the supplied date, inspection type, and applicable usage reading.
  3. Select the supplied training reference for the finding.
  4. Write an observation-based fault description.
  5. Review status and any required escalation using the exercise criteria.

Phase 3: distinguish repair from a plan

One scenario may state that a repair has been completed and verified; another may state that a part is requested or the cause remains unknown. Your record must preserve that difference. Record completed work only when the scenario provides it. Keep unresolved faults open and identify the next responsible action without inventing a signature, test result, or release decision.

  1. Read exactly what corrective work the scenario says occurred.
  2. Enter completed work and verification only when supplied.
  3. Identify pending parts, tests, or work without marking them complete.
  4. Leave required certifications to the designated role.
  5. Check that every entry can be traced to a stated fact.

Phase 4: deliver a useful handoff

Brief the next person using five points: which vehicle, which inspection, what was found, what was completed, and what remains pending. Mention uncertainties and incomplete checks. A good handoff allows the next maintainer or supervisor to act without guessing. The course’s completion badge records learning activity only; it is not a qualification, equipment-status decision, or dispatch authorization.

  1. Identify the fictional vehicle and inspection.
  2. Summarize new and changed faults by location and observation.
  3. State any completed corrective work and verification.
  4. State unresolved work and required review.
  5. Confirm that the worksheet reaches the next responsible role.

Use the course as a rehearsal for supervised work

Review any missed questions and repeat the demonstrations until you can explain the reason for each step. Then apply that reasoning with the actual vehicle TM, current forms instructions, qualified supervision, and authorized maintenance system. Successful training means knowing when you have enough evidence to proceed and when you must stop, look up an instruction, or ask the responsible person.

  1. Review feedback for every missed concept.
  2. Repeat the form exercise with a different supplied scenario.
  3. Explain the difference between an observation, diagnosis, corrective action, and release.
  4. Locate the real publications and local workflow before actual work.
  5. Practice on equipment only within your training and authorization.

Keep these habits

  • Keep every record entry traceable to an observation or completed action.
  • Uncertainty and incomplete work must remain visible.
  • Course completion prepares you for supervised practice; it does not authorize maintenance or operation.

Read alongside: The technical manual for the exact vehicle and configuration; DA Pam 750–8 — The Army Maintenance Management System (TAMMS) Users Manual; DA Form 2404 — Equipment Inspection and Maintenance Worksheet; GCSS-Army — training and system procedures; Dispatch records — DA Form 5987-E / DD Form 1970, as applicable.
