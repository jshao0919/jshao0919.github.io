Scope of this course

A broad introduction to publicly discussed Iranian UAS, including historical families and reported names. It is not a complete or current inventory. Recognition examples are educational, and a shape or name alone cannot establish origin, operator, intent, or exact variant.

Counter-UAS instruction covers safety, reporting, uncertainty, and authorized response principles. It does not prescribe engagement ranges, weapon selection, or rules of engagement. The sensor-network example is fictional and is not a description of Camp Arifjan or any installation.

How to use this course

  1. Open a model study page. Read its design vocabulary and original source captions.
  2. Use the first three lessons to distinguish source claims, visible features, and unresolved questions. Continue through the course and mark lessons complete.
  3. Change one variable at a time in each lab and predict the result.
  4. Use module practice, then take the full assessment. Review every explanation.

Allow several sessions for the reading, atlas, and labs. Completion is a self-study record, not an operational qualification.

What you will learn

  • Separate an observation from an identification claim.
  • Understand broad airframe layouts and naming ambiguities.
  • Read public image captions, dates, creator credits, and reuse terms critically.
  • Explain why CFAR adapts a threshold to background noise.
  • Recognize when sensors provide repeated rather than independent evidence.
  • Report uncertainty, follow official alerts, and leave hazardous debris alone.

Science lab / 01

A threshold that listens to the background

Synthetic data

Constant False Alarm Rate (CFAR) adjusts a detection threshold using nearby noise estimates. A threshold crossing is a detection candidate, not an aircraft identification.

Dimensionless classroom values. These are not radar settings, distances, or detection-performance predictions.

━ Navy: sampled power━ Coral: local threshold● Teal: threshold crossing

Inspect one cell

The center cell is tested. Guard cells are skipped to reduce contamination by the same echo. Reference (training) cells estimate the local background.

Derive the threshold

T = α × mean(reference powers)
α = N × (PFA−1/N − 1)

N is the total number of reference cells on both sides. This formula assumes independent exponential noise powers, a single-look detector, and homogeneous reference cells. It operates on linear power, not decibels.

At a background transition or near another echo, those assumptions can fail. “Constant” is a model-based design goal; it is not a guarantee in arbitrary clutter.

Try three experiments
  1. Keep the noise fixed. Lower the requested false-alarm probability. Watch the threshold rise and explain why a weak echo may no longer cross it.
  2. Switch to the background-change scene. Compare thresholds on either side of the transition. Explain why a single local-noise model becomes questionable.
  3. Switch to nearby echoes. Change guard width and inspect which reference cells contain strong samples. Explain why neighbouring echoes can bias the mean.

Counts in a single 96-cell scene are not measured detection probabilities. The highlighted injected cells are teaching labels unavailable to a real detector.

More measurements. Better questions.

What the combined picture can say

Three checks before combining
  1. Do these measurements concern the same event?
  2. Are time stamps, coordinate frames, and uncertainty compatible?
  3. Are these independent observations, or the same report repeated?

Use authentication, access control, health monitoring, audit logs, and clear human responsibility. Retain disagreement and missing data rather than silently smoothing them away.

Why independence matters: a toy probability model

Hypothesis H: a manufactured test object is present. These numbers are invented for learning; they are not assigned to any sensor or aircraft. A likelihood ratio compares how likely an observation is with H versus without H.

If the reports share a source or common error, multiplying their individual likelihood ratios can overstate confidence. Without a joint model, this lab intentionally does not calculate a combined probability.

Reference & responsible practice

A careful report is useful evidence

Observe → report → follow official guidance

  1. Put personal safety first. Follow official alerts, posted shelter instructions, and designated local authorities.
  2. Separate facts from interpretation. Record the time, vantage point, visible form, behaviour, and limitations only if safe and authorized.
  3. Use the approved reporting channel. Say what is uncertain and whether a report is firsthand or repeated.
  4. Leave intervention to authorized personnel. Legal authority, identification requirements, airspace coordination, and risk to people govern any response.
  5. Keep clear of debris. Do not touch, move, collect, or approach it for photographs. Notify the appropriate emergency or explosive-hazard authority through local procedures.

Counter-UAS response framework

Detection, tracking, classification, identification, and authorization are distinct decisions. A sensor alert alone does not settle any of the later decisions.

Distance alone cannot prescribe a weapon or response. Applicable law, command authority, approved rules, airspace deconfliction, civilian risk, and validated system limitations all matter. This course provides no engagement matrix or site-specific tactics.

At an installation, consult the current approved local procedures for alert meanings, reporting channels, shelter arrangements, and authorized responsibilities. Public U.S. agency guidance below provides educational context; it does not establish legal authority in Kuwait.

Practice writing an observation

Use this fictional exercise: you briefly see an indistinct fixed-wing shape in a training video. A colleague calls it a specific model, but you cannot see the tail or markings. Write a report that distinguishes what you saw from what you were told.

Fictional training notes only. Do not enter installation details, personal data, or operational observations. This form is not saved or transmitted.

Example of a calibrated report

“In the training video, I saw a fixed-wing shape briefly. I could not resolve its tail, markings, or dimensions. A colleague suggested a model name; I cannot independently confirm it. The image alone does not establish the operator, origin, or intent.”

Useful reports identify the source and limits of each claim. This exercise is not a real incident report.
