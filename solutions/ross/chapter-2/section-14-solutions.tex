\section{Series}
\label{sec:series}
For a sequence $(a_n)$, its $N$th partial sum is
$S_N=\sum_{n=1}^{N}a_n$. The series $\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a_n$
converges precisely when $(S_N)$ has a finite real limit. A limit of
$+\infty$ or $-\infty$ is divergence, even though it specifies a
direction of divergence.

The necessary condition $a_n\to0$ is not sufficient for convergence.
The Comparison, Ratio, and Root Tests supply sufficient conditions
in the situations specified below. When terms are nonnegative,
convergence and absolute convergence are the same assertion.
Unless a lower index is explicitly given, the series in the catalogs
begin at $n=1$; changing finitely many initial terms does not affect
convergence, as proved in Exercise~14.9. The logarithm is the natural
logarithm.
