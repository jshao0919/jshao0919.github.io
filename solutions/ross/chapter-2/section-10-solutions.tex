\section{Monotone sequences and Cauchy sequences}
\label{sec:monotone-cauchy}
An increasing sequence satisfies $s_n\le s_{n+1}$; a decreasing
sequence satisfies $s_n\ge s_{n+1}$. Both definitions permit equality.
A sequence is bounded if all its terms lie between two fixed real numbers.

The Cauchy condition says that, for every $\varepsilon>0$, there is
one threshold $N$ such that
\[
 m>N,\ n>N\quad\Longrightarrow\quad \abs{s_m-s_n}<\varepsilon.
\]
It controls every pair of late terms. Merely making successive
differences small is not enough, as Exercise 10.6 explains.
The solutions first justify convergence and then identify a limit.
